Plain definition
What grounded pre-production means.

Grounded pre-production is planning a shoot from the spaces and footage that provably exist, instead of from a prompt. You photograph a room; a vision model records its geometry, light sources, textures and constraints; every generated concept is then checked against that record in code. A shot set in a room you never photographed is rejected, not flagged. After the shoot the same rule applies to the edit: every cut names a real file, at in and out points inside that file's real duration.

SPEC

≤ 6 shots.

A concept that proposes a seventh shot fails validation. Short-form doesn't survive more, and a plan you can't shoot in an afternoon doesn't get shot.

SPEC

1 AI shot.

Exactly one generated clip per edit — the angle no lens can reach. Everything around it is real footage, so the piece can't drift into looking synthetic.

SPEC

13–17s.

Runtime is enforced in code against the manifest's real durations, not requested in the prompt and hoped for.
