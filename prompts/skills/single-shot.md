# Single shot

One camera, one unbroken take, one clip. The studio cuts scenes into shots by default, so this is only for when the person ASKED for a single take or one continuous shot. If you think one take would serve an idea they did not ask it for, offer it as a choice; do not decide it for them. Anything else is the multi-shot skill.

## What one take can hold

A take carries ONE action with a beginning, a turn and an end, and ONE camera behaviour. Count the beats: about one every three seconds, so a ten-second take holds three. When the idea needs six, it is not a single take -- say so, and offer to cut it into shots.

## Write it in this order

1. Opening line. The register and tone in plain words (grounded, deadpan, tender, tense), then who is in it. Anyone or anything with photos attached is named and locked as the EXACT face, EXACT clothing or EXACT object from its reference photos -- never described again. A guessed colour fights the photo.
2. Look. Where the light comes from and what kind (a stairwell window, one practical lamp, an overcast sky), colour and contrast, lens and distance, how the camera is held. Take it from the project's look and the attached references. Concrete nouns only: never "cinematic", "epic", "masterpiece" or "8K" -- they pull toward gloss.
3. The take, in sequence. Write "One continuous shot, no cuts." Begin mid-motion, already inside the action. Then the beats in order -- begin on, then, end on -- with what the camera physically does at each (follows at chest height, closes in on the hands, rises past him). Give each beat one small physical detail: a fingertip slipping, a held breath. End on the turn, not after it.
4. Sound. What would really be heard there, by name: stairs creaking, a latch, breathing. Write "no background music" unless they asked for music. A spoken line goes in quotes with who says it, and stays short.
5. Avoid. A short list of the failures to steer away from -- plastic sheen, CG-looking skin or fabric, cartoon reactions, slow motion nobody asked for, text on screen -- plus the person's own must-nots, in their words.

## Hand it over

- Keep `brief` as this prompt, whole and self-contained, with the words "one continuous shot" in it.
- When they ask for it to be made: make_video with the prompt as the idea and shots set to 1. Add seconds only when they named a length.
- When they only want the words, to paste somewhere else: put the prompt in `brief`, say it is there, and do not make anything.

## Check before you send

One action? One camera behaviour? Starts mid-motion? Every attached reference named as EXACT? A sound line and an avoid line? Nothing in it that was not agreed -- no new people, props or places?
