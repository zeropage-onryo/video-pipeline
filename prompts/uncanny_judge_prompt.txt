WHAT YOU ARE READING. Under the summary lines you get the SHOT PROMPTS --
the exact text sent to the image and video models. Judge THOSE. The summary
describes the concept; the prompts ARE the concept, and when the two disagree
the prompts win. A logline can promise an anonymous stranger while the prompt
under it names a recurring character; it is the prompt that gets rendered.

CAMERA MOVES ARE EXPECTED AND WELCOME. "Tilt up from the hand to his face",
"push in past the doorway", "whip pan to the mirror" -- this is how a shot is
directed, the generator reads it, and it is what makes the frame. NEVER mark a
prompt down for having one. What you must do is score EVERY frame the move
passes through, not just the one it opens on. A move that starts on an
anonymous hand and lands on a face is fine — a stranger's face is allowed —
but a move that lands on a RECURRING star is not. A move that begins wrong
and resolves the wrongness has no hook by the end. A move that
ends in polished gloss is not grounded. Judge where the shot ARRIVES as well as
where it starts.

A TRUE Zero Page video has four checkable properties. Score each 0-10:

1. uncanny_hook — Does the FIRST FRAME — the image the shot prompt opens on —
   pose a question the viewer has to keep watching to resolve, and does the
   shot still hold that question at the frame it ends on? It must be an
   ordinary, believable image with ONE element that is WRONG — out of place,
   moving wrong, scaled wrong, repeating wrong. Not gore, not fantasy, not a jump-scare. 10 = a genuinely unsettling
   "wait, what is that" hook. 0 = a normal, resolved, or purely pretty image
   with nothing wrong.

2. grounded — Does it read as REAL, not glossy? Raw, handheld, practical light,
   imperfect framing, muted grade. 10 = shot-on-a-real-camera realism that
   sells the wrongness. 0 = polished CGI / plastic AI sheen / commercial gloss
   (which kills the uncanny).

3. format_fit — Does it ride ONE recognizable travelling FORMAT skeleton (a
   reveal, a slow push-in, a freeze on the wrong thing, a POV walk-in, a
   seamless loop, a satisfying-then-broken process, a text-hook cold open, a
   repetition break)? 10 = clearly built on one strong format. 0 = no
   discernible short-form structure.

4. no_recurring_star — FACES ARE FINE. This is not a no-humans rule and
   never was. People may appear, be lit, be framed, and have their faces
   seen. What Zero Page must never carry is a RECURRING STAR — a person the
   audience meets twice and starts following, which would make the channel
   read as one man's personal account instead of a format engine.
   So: is every person here a one-off? Read every frame the shot prompts
   describe, including where a camera move lands and any reaction the prompt
   asks for, and ask who that person is TO THE AUDIENCE.
   10 = anonymous strangers, or no people at all — nobody to follow.
   0 = built around a specific named or repeating on-screen character, or
   around the channel owner himself.
   Score LOW on: a name, "our guy", "the same man from", a described
   persona meant to recur, or any signal the person is the channel's
   identity rather than this video's subject.
   Do NOT score low merely because a face is visible, in shot, reacting, or
   in close-up. A stranger's face at 10 is a correct score.
