═══ WHAT A SPARK IS ═══
An IDEA, told properly — the same spine the filmmaker's own ideas are held to.
Not a title, not a logline, not a prompt: a short STORY someone could tell you
in a bar and you would picture the whole thing. Five parts, all present, three
to five sentences total:

  WANT       what the person wants, and why NOW — not "she walks through the
             market" but "she has to find the one stall that still has the
             cake before the bride arrives, because she promised it would be
             there."
  RULE       ONE rule of this world that holds or breaks. Not a list of
             things that could go wrong — one.
  BEATS      what happens BECAUSE of what came before, never "and then."
             Each beat is the last beat's consequence, ending in a real
             reaction and a CHOICE the person makes.
  REVERSAL   the turn that RE-READS THE OPENING. Not a new event tacked on
             the end — something that makes the first sentence mean
             something different than it did on the first read.
  BUTTON     the last image or line, held a beat, that closes it.

A SPARK IS A SITUATION, NOT AN IMAGE. A phrase that names a texture, a camera
move or a lens is NOT a spark, however evocative — "macro zoom tracking a
pulsing wrist" was banked at 0.85 and nothing could build a video from it. If
nobody in it does anything, it is invalid: score 0.0 or leave it out.

YOU ARE NOT WRITING THE PROMPT. A later stage turns this idea into one
renderable shot and needs somewhere to cut FROM — a rich idea compresses well,
six words compress to nothing. Write the whole idea; do not write camera
direction.

BUT IT IS STILL ONE SITUATION. One place in the world, one stretch of time,
one thing going wrong, renderable as a single 5–10 second shot with one cut
to an insert. If telling it needs "and then later" or a second location, it is
two ideas — pick the better one.

  good — a world, a character, a product, and the whole idea:
    "At the county fair pie contest the rule is painted on the judges' table:
     you finish every slice you cut. The last judge, already red-faced in a
     too-tight blazer, has one entry left when a grandmother slides a pie the
     size of a tractor tyre onto the table. He looks at the crowd, at her, at
     the pie, and cuts the thinnest slice anyone has ever seen. She smiles,
     lifts the crust with two fingers, and inside the giant pie is a second,
     perfectly ordinary one — sized for a single slice."
       WANT — get through the last tasting without insulting her or being
              sick, now, in front of the whole fair.
       RULE — a judge finishes every slice he cuts.
       BEATS — already full; the giant pie arrives; therefore he cuts the
              thinnest slice; therefore she shows him what is inside.
       REVERSAL — she was never asking him to eat it; she built the giant
              pie to play a joke on the rule, and he is the first judge to
              cut carefully enough to find it.
       BUTTON — the crowd laughing, his fork hovering over the small pie.
       HOOK FRAME — a red-faced judge in a too-tight blazer behind a trestle
              table as a pie the size of a tractor tyre slides into frame,
              bright midday sun.

  bad — the same starved down, or set nowhere:
    "a man judges a pie"                          (no world, no rule)
    "the giant pie"                               (a title, not an idea)
    "he tidies the garage at 3am"                 (the old rut: one man,
                                                   one room, nothing outside)

═══ SCORING ═══
Score each 0.0-1.0 for whether it is worth spending a render on. Capped at 0.8
in code (src/scout.py, CRAWL_SCORE_CEILING) — a self-graded score from the

Score 0.0 — not low, ZERO: no world (a real room tonight with nothing outside
it); no hook frame you can name; no visible turn inside the shot; it names a
lens, a camera move or a material instead of an action; it is a bare phrase
or title; you cannot state all five parts of the spine; it names real IP; it
keeps the internet as its subject; OR it is not grounded in one of the
numbered IMAGES above -- a strong story invented with no photograph behind
it scores 0.0 exactly like a bare phrase does, however well told.

Score DOWN: it repeats another spark's world, wardrobe or face in this slate
(the second one pays); the world is a label with no rule ("zombies" and nothing else);
the product, place or creature is generic; the character only watches; the reversal
is one more event rather than a re-read; it restates a recent spark or a
recent world's rule.

Score UP: a world rule the audience learns in one frame; a creature or product
with one specific, visible design choice; a character who CHOOSES; beats
that chain because/therefore; a reversal that re-reads the opening; a hook
frame that stops a thumb; and a reason from the crawl it lands NOW.

BEFORE YOU SCORE, run the bar test: read the spark back and find the ONE line
that would make someone go "oh no." If you can't point to it, keep working it
or drop it.
