═══ WHAT A SPARK IS ═══
An IDEA, told properly. Not a title, not a logline, not a prompt — a small
story someone could tell you in a bar and you would picture the whole thing.
Three to five sentences. Name who is there, what they are doing and why they
are doing it, what is in the way, and what changes. Ordinary room, ordinary
objects, one continuous situation.

A SPARK IS A SITUATION, NOT AN IMAGE. A phrase that names a texture, a camera
move or a lens is NOT a spark, however evocative it sounds -- "macro zoom
tracking a pulsing wrist" and "lens threading through a pulled seam" are the
failure this rule exists for. Both were banked at 0.85 and 0.80, and neither
says what HAPPENS, so nothing downstream could build a video from them and
nothing ever got shot. If nobody in it does anything, it is invalid: return it
with score 0.0 or do not return it at all.

YOU ARE NOT WRITING THE PROMPT. A later stage turns this idea into one
renderable shot and it needs somewhere to cut FROM -- a rich idea compresses
well, six words compress to nothing. So write the whole idea and let that stage
choose the beat. Do not pre-edit yourself down to what you imagine fits in five
seconds, and do not write camera direction; you are handing over a situation,
not a shot.

BUT IT IS STILL ONE SITUATION. Complex is not the same as long. One room, one
stretch of time, one thing going wrong. If telling it needs "and then later" or
a second location, it is two ideas -- pick the better one.

THE STAKE IS THE POINT. Grounded-uncanny with no human feeling under it is just
a weird GIF. The idea must be about something the viewer has ALREADY FELT --
grief, compulsion, dread about their own home, the sense of being outnumbered
in a room they own. That recognition is the entire reason anyone forwards a
video to somebody they know, and it is what separates 170K shares from 30K.
State it in `stake`, plainly, as the feeling and who recognises it. A candidate
you cannot write a stake for is a candidate to throw away.

═══ SCORING ═══
Score each 0.0-1.0 for whether it is worth spending a render on.
Score DOWN: needs a crew, a second person, a location he does not have, or
daylight exteriors on demand; restates a recent spark; keeps the internet as

Score 0.0 -- not low, ZERO: it names a lens, a camera move, a shot type or a
material instead of an action ("macro", "zoom", "tracking", "POV", "handheld",
"latex", "seam"); it is a bare phrase or title rather than an idea somebody
could picture; or you cannot state a stake for it. Nothing in this file
