═══ WHAT A SPARK IS ═══
An IDEA, told properly — the same spine the filmmaker's own ideas are held to
(the idea-agent skill was rewritten around it 2026-09-03, and this crawl was
still handing back moments wearing a trend headline as a costume after that
landed everywhere else — this section is that fix). Not a title, not a
logline, not a prompt: a short STORY someone could tell you in a bar and you
would picture the whole thing. Five parts, all present, three to five
sentences total:

  WANT       what the person wants, and why TONIGHT specifically — not "he
             brushes his teeth" but "he needs the cup to look the way it
             always did."
  RULE       ONE obstacle, stated as a rule that holds or breaks. Not a list
             of things that could go wrong — one.
  BEATS      what happens BECAUSE of what came before, never "and then."
             Each beat is the last beat's consequence, ending in a real
             reaction and a CHOICE the person makes — not something that
             just happens to them.
  REVERSAL   the turn that RE-READS THE OPENING. Not a new event tacked on
             the end — something that makes the first sentence mean
             something different than it did on the first read.
  BUTTON     the last image or line, held a beat, that closes it.

A SPARK IS A SITUATION, NOT AN IMAGE. A phrase that names a texture, a camera
move or a lens is NOT a spark, however evocative it sounds -- "macro zoom
tracking a pulsing wrist" and "lens threading through a pulled seam" are the
failure this rule exists for. Both were banked at 0.85 and 0.80, and neither
says what HAPPENS, so nothing downstream could build a video from them and
nothing ever got shot. If nobody in it does anything, it is invalid: return it
with score 0.0 or do not return it at all.

YOU ARE NOT WRITING THE PROMPT. A later stage turns this idea into one
renderable shot and it needs somewhere to cut FROM -- a rich idea compresses
well, six words compress to nothing. So write the whole idea and let that stage
choose the beat. Do not pre-edit yourself down to what you imagine fits in five
seconds, and do not write camera direction; you are handing over a situation,
not a shot.

BUT IT IS STILL ONE SITUATION. Complex is not the same as long. One room, one
stretch of time, one thing going wrong. If telling it needs "and then later" or
a second location, it is two ideas -- pick the better one.

  good — the whole idea, and you can see it (this is the skill's own worked
  example, told as a spark):
    "Every night since the funeral he brushes his teeth and then wets her
     toothbrush too, so the cup looks the way it always did. Tonight her
     brush is already wet when he reaches for it. He stands there holding
     it, dripping, and checks the obvious — the shower, the hall, the
     bathroom door he knows he locked. Nothing. So he dries her brush
     carefully on the towel, puts it back dry, and turns off the light.
     From the dark hallway, the tap turns on."
       WANT — keep the ritual so nothing about the apartment admits she's
              gone, tonight the same as every other night this has worked.
       RULE — he is the only one who wets that brush. One rule, breakable
              exactly one way.
       BEATS — he wets it, routine; therefore he reaches for it dry,
              expecting his own last night's work; therefore finding it
              already wet forces the check; therefore finding nothing
              forces the choice to dry it and let it go rather than wait up.
       REVERSAL — the tap turning on reframes the whole ritual: he wasn't
              keeping her presence alive alone. Something else just took
              a turn in it.
       BUTTON — the tap, alone, in the dark, right after he decided to
              stop looking.
       ONE-SENTENCE RETELL — a grieving man's nightly ritual for his dead
              wife gets performed back at him by something in the dark.

  bad — the same idea starved down to a label:
    "a man wets his dead wife's toothbrush"      (a premise: no rule, no reversal)
    "setting the table for one too many"          (a title, not an idea)
    "locking a door that is already locked"       (a premise with nothing in it)

═══ SCORING ═══
Score each 0.0-1.0 for whether it is worth spending a render on. This is
capped at 0.8 in code regardless of what you return here (src/scout.py,
CRAWL_SCORE_CEILING) — a self-graded score from the same call that wrote the
spark should never be able to outrank a direction the filmmaker typed
himself, which always scores 1.0.

Score DOWN: needs a crew, a second person, a location he does not have, or
daylight exteriors on demand; restates a recent spark; keeps the internet as
its subject; or its only argument is that something is trending.

Score 0.0 -- not low, ZERO: it names a lens, a camera move, a shot type or a
material instead of an action ("macro", "zoom", "tracking", "POV", "handheld",
"latex", "seam"); it is a bare phrase or title rather than an idea somebody
could picture; or you cannot state all five parts of the spine for it — a
want with no rule, beats that only chain with "and then," or a reversal that
is just one more event tacked on rather than something that changes how the
opening reads. Nothing in this file penalised the first of those before
2026-08-31, which is how four camera specs came to sit in the bank scored
0.80 and above; nothing penalised the second before 2026-09-02, which is how
a night of one-line premises read as thin even when every one was a real
situation; and nothing penalised the third — a moment with a feeling but no
turn — before this rewrite.

BEFORE YOU SCORE, run the bar test: read the spark back and find the ONE line
that would make someone go "oh no." If you can't point to it, this is a
premise, not a story yet — keep working it or drop it.

Score UP: a visible physical action; beats that are causally linked
(because/therefore, never "and then"); a reversal that actually
recontextualises the want or the rule, not just a final beat; a button, held;
and the crawl gives a reason it lands NOW.
